# Executive Summary: UAC Care System Capacity Analytics

## Decision question
Is the combined CBP–HHS care pipeline experiencing rising care responsibility, sustained inflow pressure, or signs of relief?

## What the dashboard monitors
- **Total Children Under Care:** combined CBP and HHS active load
- **Net Intake Pressure:** CBP-to-HHS transfers minus HHS discharges
- **Care Load Volatility:** stability of the recent operating environment
- **Backlog Accumulation:** cumulative positive-pressure periods
- **Discharge Offset Ratio:** the ability of discharges to relieve incoming transfers

## Current dataset snapshot
The analysis covers **720 valid observations** from **2023-01-12 through 2025-12-21**. The latest recorded combined system load is **2,502 children**. The historical maximum is **11,762 children**.

## Recommended management approach
**Green:** stable or declining load with discharge capacity keeping pace.  
**Amber:** sustained positive net intake or a rising 7-day system-load trend. Review staffing and available beds.  
**Red:** prolonged positive net intake combined with elevated load or deteriorating discharge relief. Activate surge-capacity and sponsor-placement acceleration planning.

## Important caveat
The dataset does not include a direct bed-capacity denominator. Load indicators therefore identify *relative operational pressure*, not formal occupancy or a guaranteed threshold of overcrowding.

## Bottom line
A centralized dashboard can shift response from reactive reporting to continuous monitoring. The recommended operating model is to review daily signals while making staffing and shelter decisions primarily from sustained 7-day and 14-day trends plus the 30-day HHS-care forecast.
